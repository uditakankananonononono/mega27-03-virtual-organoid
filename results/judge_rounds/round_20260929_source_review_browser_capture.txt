The existing biological result also does not currently justify reopening the project through the same high/low responder question.

I would treat the following as the bounded source/novelty review, not as a review of the 93-page manuscript.

The important distinction is that 0.738672 is not evidence of a successful independent segmentation method merely because it is close to 0.76. If 0.76 is an aggregate benchmark from a different source/protocol, it cannot be treated as an independent matched-object comparison. Conversely, the withdrawn AP result cannot remain part of the evidence simply because it was once measured.

So I would not relax either HOLD gate on the basis of proximity to the benchmark.

2. Strongest genuinely different biological direction

There is one direction I would investigate before abandoning the biological question entirely:

Can the transcriptional state associated with CFTR-modulator response be explained by epithelial cell-state composition rather than a single responder gene?

That is materially different from asking:

“What genes distinguish the 6 high responders from the 6 low responders?”

The latter is essentially a re-analysis of the published responder grouping and is especially weak here because the source paper apparently did not find an interpretable separating gene.

The more interesting falsifiable question is whether response-associated transcriptional variation tracks a specific cellular-state composition or differentiation program, rather than a conventional DEG.

For example, the hypothesis could be:

H1: ETI-response-associated transcriptional differences in F508del intestinal organoids are better explained by variation in epithelial cell-state composition/state proportions than by a reproducible single-gene responder signature.

The corresponding null would be:

H0: response-associated expression variation cannot be explained beyond donor-level/global transcriptional variation by reproducible cell-state composition.

This is biologically meaningful because CFTR function is strongly dependent on epithelial state, while organoid cultures can differ substantially in their cellular composition. But I would not call this a viable project yet. The data have to support the necessary unit of analysis.

Candidate source: GSE263022

Source-confirmed from your supplied description:

12 F508del-homozygous donors.

6 high and 6 low ETI-FIS responders.

Seven RNA-seq wells/timepoints per donor.

Published source reports no interpretable separating gene.

No per-organoid baseline/end-area linkage.

Critical limitation: seven RNA-seq observations per donor do not automatically provide seven independent biological replicates.

The useful experimental unit is initially donor, with wells/timepoints nested inside donor. Treating the 84 observations as independent would be pseudoreplication.

A defensible analysis would therefore need:

donor-level response labels fixed before analysis;

well/timepoint retained as nested repeated observations;

no random train/test splitting of wells from the same donor;

leave-one-donor-out validation if prediction is attempted;

cell-state/pathway scores computed without using the response label to select features;

a permutation test that permutes donor labels, not individual RNA-seq wells;

a comparison against donor-only/global-expression baselines.

And critically, no claim of individual-organoid swelling mechanism can be made from this source because you do not have linked per-organoid baseline→endpoint measurements.

GSE164638: potentially more mechanistic, but currently weaker as an eligibility candidate

Your description makes this interesting but substantially less clean:

mouse intestinal organoid single-cell data;

published forskolin-induced stretch state;

different forskolin/CFTR-inhibitor timepoints;

no verified independent treatment-culture replication.

A potentially novel hypothesis is:

H1: forskolin-induced epithelial state transitions contain a conserved cellular-state program that distinguishes CFTR-dependent swelling from nonspecific organoid expansion.

But that requires cross-species/state correspondence, which creates a substantial inference burden.

I would not accept “the mouse scRNA-seq clusters correlate with the human ETI responders” as the primary claim. That would be too easy to turn into post-hoc narrative.

A much stronger test would require a preregistered state signature derived independently from one source and tested without re-fitting on the other.

Status: candidate lead, not yet eligible.

4. Eligibility checks: ranked

I would impose these in roughly this order.

Tier 1 — hard stop checks

1. Exact image provenance

For every evaluation image, establish:

source accession → sample/field ID → original image → derivative/crop/augmentation history

Any unresolved ancestry should keep the image out of the independent test set.

2. Train/development/test disjointness

No image, crop, mask, field, or near-duplicate can cross the boundary.

This is especially important for microscopy because random image splitting can make apparently independent images effectively identical at the field/donor level.

3. Donor independence

For biological analyses, define donor as the highest-level biological replicate unless the experimental design explicitly supports another unit.

Never count:

12 donors × 7 wells = 84 independent samples.

It is 12 donors with repeated measurements.

4. Culture/well independence

Determine whether wells came from independent cultures or merely technical/temporal replicates of the same culture.

A different well is not automatically a biological replicate.

5. Object independence

For segmentation:

multiple organoids from one field are correlated;

multiple crops from one organoid are correlated;

multiple frames from one acquisition are correlated.

AP should therefore be reported at the appropriate independent acquisition unit, not merely by pooling thousands of objects.

Tier 2 — benchmark contamination

6. Method-selection blindness

Before touching the sealed test set, freeze:

preprocessing;

model;

thresholding;

post-processing;

augmentation;

hyperparameters;

object filters.

Otherwise the test set has become a development set.

7. Literature-model contamination

If comparing against the published Mask R-CNN 0.76, determine whether the comparison is actually apples-to-apples:

same images?

same annotation definition?

same object inclusion rules?

same IoU threshold?

same instance-matching procedure?

same field population?

An aggregate published AP is not automatically a valid comparator.

8. Public-model contamination

If OrgaSegment/OrgLine or another published model was trained using the eventual test images, those images cannot establish an independent benchmark for that model or descendants of that model.

Tier 3 — biological validity

9. Donor-level permutation

For any responder-group hypothesis, permutation must occur at the donor level.

Not cell, well, read, or timepoint.

10. Leave-one-donor-out validation

If prediction is attempted, the held-out unit should be the donor.

Anything weaker risks measuring donor recognition rather than biological generalization.

11. Timepoint nesting

Timepoints must remain nested inside donor/culture.

Randomly distributing timepoints across train/test is unacceptable.

12. Treatment nesting

ETI/FSK/control conditions must remain linked to their experimental culture structure.

Do not treat each treatment observation as a new biological replicate.

5. What would make the GSE263022 direction genuinely interesting?

I would require a very narrow preregistration.

For example:

Primary unit: donor.

Primary hypothesis: a prespecified epithelial-state score explains responder-group variation beyond donor-level/global expression structure.

Controls:

donor permutation;

batch/well covariates;

timepoint;

library-depth normalization;

global-expression principal components;

a randomly selected gene-set negative control;

a label-independent cell-state signature;

leave-one-donor-out analysis.

And importantly, require an effect-size criterion, not simply p < .05.

Otherwise, with only 12 donors, a large number of pathway/state scores could generate an attractive nominal result by chance.

The key negative-result rule

If the state-composition hypothesis fails under donor-level permutation and leave-one-donor-out testing, stop.

Do not descend into:

pathway → gene → transcript → individual donor subgroup

until something passes.

That would recreate the same analytical flexibility that makes the original high/low responder analysis unattractive.

Bottom-line verdict

1. HOLD is correct. Neither the original matched-object AP gate nor the acquisition-independent AP gate has a presently admissible success.

2. I do not see a currently verified public dataset that justifies claiming a new organoid segmentation result. OrgaExtractor is the most obvious thing to audit, but until the 28 evaluation pairs and their ancestry are independently verified, it is only a candidate.

3. The strongest biological lead is not another high-vs-low responder re-analysis. It is a preregistered test of whether cellular epithelial-state composition/state transitions explain responder-associated variation, preferably beginning with GSE263022 at the donor level and using GSE164638 only as an independently defined mechanistic/state reference if its experimental structure survives audit.

4. If GSE263022 cannot support a valid donor-level state analysis and GSE164638 cannot establish independent treatment/culture units, then I would call the current public-data source pool insufficient rather than manufacture a rescue.

That last outcome is scientifically preferable to converting the failed swelling gate, withdrawn AP result, and contaminated image sources into a new claim.
