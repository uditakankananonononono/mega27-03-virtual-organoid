# Missing-track sensitivity of the independent one-plate FIS demo

Pre-registration 6a0aed7 fixed an attrition audit and a 5th/95th-percentile adverse-outcome envelope before this computation. One public FIS_image_analysis demo plate, one class-II CFTR donor/genotype, 32 object CSVs. The earlier global small/large starting-area thresholds were held fixed.

M1, among *all* positive-area baseline objects: forskolin-only (vehicle) small paired 239/253 (94.5%) and large 260/287 (90.6%); VX-809/VX-770 small 251/272 (92.3%) and large 230/283 (81.3%). The large objects lost despite unique baseline labels had median area 11,932 μm² in control and 11,571 μm² in combination, vs retained large medians 9,541 and 9,827 μm² respectively. Thus dropout depends on size, especially in the drug condition; analyzing only successful tracks is selectively conditioned. The 130 missing baseline labels and 205 duplicate endpoint rows are not evidence of zero swelling.

M2, each baseline-positive object without a usable paired outcome receives an artificial 5th or 95th percentile of *observed* matched log-swelling from its same dose/treatment/size cell. In the adversarial assignment (drug-large and control-small low, drug-small and control-large high), six of eight dose effects remain positive and the median is +0.047 log units; best-case median +0.213. This meets the preregistered `ROBUST_WITHIN_ENVELOPE` flag, but it is merely conditional on the chosen observed 5th/95th quantiles. More extreme or size-specific unobserved outcomes could erase the sign, and there is no evidence that observed tails bound missing outcomes. No p-value or patient-general confidence claim is attached. Exact per-dose bounds and cell counts: `results/external_fis_missingtracks.json`.

Net verdict: the observed one-plate trend survives a stated, limited missingness envelope but material size-dependent attrition remains. The original donor x plate x dose stress test on a separate dataset still fails (9/12 donor medians positive, p=.073). No established discovery or diagnosis.
