# Candidate-scene-group-disjoint eval: what 0.750403 can and cannot show

27 September 2026. The frozen image-only graph and reassignment, from commits b8e5132 and db30071, sent 180, 37 and 14 images to train, validation and eval. The new 60-epoch checkpoint was initialized afresh and chosen by weighted validation loss; postprocessing defaults were frozen in 6b23675 before the reassigned eval was scored. The per-image author AP50 mean in `results/group_disjoint_seg/exploratory_eval.json` is 0.7504031573, with image-bootstrap interval 0.68934947-0.81072153. This is an exploratory result on already seen files, not a prospective independent benchmark.

The reassignment protects candidate scene groups from crossing *these three* new partitions. It does not create 14 independent acquisition groups. Ten of the 14 eval images are singleton components at cosine >=0.95; the other four are spread across two groups, one with the original eval file and a moved train file, one with the original eval file and a moved val file. These are proxy groups, not donor or field identifiers. All 231 source images and annotations were known in the project before scoring, and the two moved-to-eval images had source filenames in the original training/validation partition. At least one 0.95 graph flag has a visually different layout, showing over-grouping; missed same-donor or same-acquisition relationships can also survive the graph. Images in one donor or plate may not be exchangeable, so the image-bootstrap CI is conditional and may be too narrow. The numerical 0.750403-versus-0.761108 difference cannot estimate leakage bias: training membership, weights, checkpoint selection and postprocessing differ.

A causal leakage test would need acquisition identifiers plus at least one untouched donor/plate/field-cohort annotation sample, with weights and transformations fixed before its labels are inspected. A fair leading-tool comparison would additionally run released author weights/predictions on precisely the same images with paired AP50 under the same mask/instance policy, and quantify between-acquisition uncertainty. Neither exists in this release. Retain the current data as a reproducible integrity exercise and do not call the original threshold crossing a benchmark win.
