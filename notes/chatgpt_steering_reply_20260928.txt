I am steering two public-data computational biology projects, with limited compute. Please suggest one or two genuinely creative, testable NEXT directions per project, not a generic laundry list. Treat these as hypotheses and name nearest prior art or search terms to check. Do not claim a positive result or propose hiding failures. Prefer low-compute, preregistrable analyses of already public material, and separate a method contribution from a biology claim.

Given the two projects you’ve been developing most recently, I’d treat the next steps as two different kinds of contribution: one centered on a new network-level method for metabolic fragility, and one centered on a new comparative biological test for Turritopsis. I would not count either as a discovery until the preregistered analysis survives its controls.

1. Objective-fragility / genome-scale metabolic models
Direction A — Fragility under objective uncertainty, not just biomass failure

Hypothesis: A metabolic intervention can appear “fragile” only because the model's chosen biomass objective creates a narrow failure mode. A more interesting quantity would be the stability of an intervention's failure across a family of biologically defensible objectives.

You already have the single- and many pairwise-demand-removal landscape, so I would not simply enumerate more knockouts. Instead:

Define a small, preregistered set of alternative objectives representing distinct cellular demands.

For each intervention, calculate its minimum intervention size needed to eliminate each objective.

Quantify an objective-robust fragility profile: does the same perturbation remain catastrophic across objectives, or is its apparent fragility objective-specific?

Freeze the objective family before seeing results.

Validate the statistic on several unrelated BiGG models, rather than treating another model's identical outcome as an independent biological validation.

Potential method contribution: a formal measure of objective-robust fragility that separates structural network vulnerability from vulnerability induced by an arbitrary objective definition.

Biology claim: only afterward: particular metabolic functions may share a common failure bottleneck.

Why this is not merely MCS repackaging: MCSs already provide a mature framework for failure modes, synthetic lethality, and robustness analysis.

Direction B — A falsifiable “repairability” law

Instead of asking which pairs break the network?, ask:

When a failure occurs, how much minimal redundancy is required to restore the same function?

For every failed single perturbation or pair, enumerate a tightly bounded set of candidate compensating reactions/genes and measure the minimum repair cardinality required to restore a predefined fraction of the original objective.

That creates a two-sided object:

failure size → repair size

rather than simply a list of lethal combinations.

The genuinely interesting hypothesis would be something like:

Networks with greater structural redundancy should exhibit systematically lower median repair cardinality after comparable failures.

This is testable across models and doesn't require inventing a biological mechanism.

Method contribution: a preregistered failure–repair asymmetry statistic.

Biology claim: particular pathways exhibit unusually low/high repairability.

The important guardrail is to avoid claiming that a computational “repair” corresponds to a real genetic rescue. It is initially a model-level structural property.

Nearest prior art: MCS/synthetic-lethal literature already treats minimal intervention sets as failure certificates, and recent work is extending MCS ideas to integrated metabolic/regulatory systems.

Direction A — Rejuvenation is a trajectory, not a list of genes

Hypothesis: genes that matter to life-cycle reversal are better characterized by their temporal trajectory and coordinated switching than by their absolute differential expression at individual stages.

Use the existing public stage-resolved transcriptomes and construct, for each gene/pathway:

direction of change across the reversal;

timing of maximal change;

whether the trajectory is monotonic or transient;

whether genes within a biological module switch coherently.

Then ask a very specific preregistered question:

Are particular biological programs characterized by coordinated transient activation during the reversal rather than simply high expression in the cyst?

This is substantially different from another GO enrichment analysis. The 2021 study already performed sequential and pairwise differential-expression analyses and reported enrichment of aging/lifespan, transposable-element regulation, DNA repair and damage-response processes in the cyst.

Yes. For VO3, I would stop trying to rescue the failed 12-donor size-by-modulator claim and use public molecular data to ask a different question that remains mechanistically connected to CFTR/FIS without pretending that transcriptomics is a replication of single-organoid swelling.

I found two unusually plausible directions.

1. Can a pre-existing epithelial state explain why CF organoids differ in CFTR-modulator response?

Source-screening target: GSE263022 is a real human CF intestinal-organoid RNA-seq dataset. It contains 12 F508del/F508del organoids—six high and six low FIS responders—with and without elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor exposure over 2–6 hours. The GEO record explicitly says the study was designed around variability in FIS response among organoids with the same genotype. 
NCBI
+1

Question to preregister:

Among F508del/F508del organoids, do baseline transcriptional differences between subsequently high- and low-FIS responders concentrate in a reproducible epithelial-state program that predicts response, rather than merely reflecting CFTR genotype?

Independent biological unit: donor/patient-derived organoid line, not individual cells or sequencing reads. The six high and six low responder organoid lines are the candidate independent units.

Endpoint: a preregistered baseline molecular score, then its association with the published high-vs-low FIS responder designation. Do not turn individual cells into extra n.

Baseline comparator: untreated/baseline samples from the high- versus low-responder lines, with treatment-induced expression analyzed separately.

Potentially novel method contribution: construct a baseline state → functional-response classifier/score, but keep it deliberately tiny—e.g. pathway-level or prespecified gene-set summaries rather than a machine-learning model. The biological discovery would be whether a particular epithelial-state program is associated with FIS response.

Nearest prior art: the dataset itself is particularly close: its authors already modeled ETI-induced transcriptional changes and high/low FIS response. 
NCBI
 The broader FIS literature establishes substantial inter-individual response variation, including among people carrying the same CFTR genotype. 
PubMed
+1

Falsification test:
Use label permutation at the donor/organoid-line level, preserving treatment/time structure. A candidate association should disappear under responder-label permutation and should not be driven by one donor.

Immediate stop rule:
If the GEO metadata/raw expression cannot unambiguously establish (i) biological donor/organoid-line identity, (ii) high/low FIS designation, (iii) baseline/untreated material, and (iv) independent biological replication, stop. Do not substitute technical replicates or individual cells.

Why I like it: it directly addresses a real unresolved feature of VO3—heterogeneity among otherwise genetically matched CF organoids—without claiming to reproduce FIS.

2. Does CFTR activation produce a conserved stretch-response transcriptional state?

This one is more exploratory but potentially more creative.

Source: GSE164638, a real public single-cell RNA-seq study of mouse intestinal organoids. It contains perturbations with forskolin and CFTRinh-172 alongside controls. Importantly, the investigators report a stretch-responsive cell state that was more abundant after forskolin and less abundant after CFTR inhibition. 
NCBI

The key distinction is that this is mouse organoid single-cell transcriptomics, not human CF FIS. I would explicitly make that a cross-system hypothesis rather than quietly treating it as VO3 validation.

Question:

Is the forskolin-associated stretch-response state in intestinal organoids characterized by a compact, reproducible transcriptional program that can be tested for enrichment in independent human CF intestinal-organoid datasets?

Independent biological unit: the organoid culture/experimental replicate, with cells nested inside that unit. For the human comparison, donor/organoid line remains the independent unit.

Endpoint: a preregistered pathway/module score representing the mouse forskolin-associated stretch state, followed by enrichment/association testing in an independent human intestinal-organoid dataset such as GSE263022.

Baseline comparator: mouse untreated/DMSO organoids versus forskolin; CFTR-inhibited organoids provide the critical opposite-direction comparator.

That opposite perturbation is the interesting part. The hypothesis isn't merely:

forskolin changes genes.

It is:

forskolin and CFTR inhibition should produce opposing movement of a defined intestinal epithelial state.

That gives you a much stronger falsification structure.

Potential method contribution: a cross-species perturbation-signature transfer test: derive the signature in one system, freeze it, and test it in another without refitting.

Biology claim: only if the frozen mouse-derived signature behaves directionally as preregistered in independent human material.

Nearest prior art: the GSE164638 investigators already reported the stretch-responsive state and its opposing abundance under forskolin versus CFTR inhibition. 
NCBI
 FIS itself is well established as a CFTR-dependent swelling phenotype, including inhibition by CFTR-specific inhibitors. 
Nature

Falsification test:
Before looking at the human data, freeze the mouse-derived signature. Then test:

forskolin direction;

CFTR-inhibition direction;

random gene-set/permuted signature controls;

ideally, an unrelated intestinal perturbation as a specificity control.

A failure of the frozen signature to transfer is a negative result, not something to repair by changing its genes afterward.

Immediate stop rule:
If GSE164638's metadata do not permit a clean distinction between biological replicate, culture condition, and cell-level observation—or if the human dataset lacks compatible untreated/treated biological units—stop the cross-species claim. Do not manufacture compatibility by batch correction or cell-level pseudoreplication.
