# Post-result scene graph forensics protocol, 26 September 2026

The saved segmentation AP50 result and its split flaw were inspected before this protocol. This is a retrospective, unsupervised forensic description, **not** a new held-out evaluation or gate amendment. ChatGPT round 05 proposed a group-aware identity and leakage audit; the operational addition here is an executable image-only graph and a reusable grouping manifest.

Input: all 231 OrgaSegment Basic image files in the original train/val/eval folders, without looking at masks, model predictions, score files, or labels while constructing groups. For each, grayscale resize to 32 x 32 bilinear; standardize to zero mean and unit SD; normalize vector length. Pairwise cosine >=0.95 creates an edge across **all** images (within- and cross-split); exact SHA256 identity also creates an edge. Connected components form candidate scene groups, and transitive closure is intentional. Threshold 0.95 was already used in the earlier post-result scout and is not a prospective pre-outcome value. Sensitivity report at 0.97 and 0.99 is descriptive, not a way to choose the 'best' group after outcomes. Store all edge names, connected components, split membership, image and manifest hashes. Do not inspect AP50 or adjust the graph based on performance.

Negative control: compare actual cross-split flagged pair scores with a fixed seeded sample of cross-split pairs; this measures enrichment of anomalously close frames, not independent scene identity or valid p-values. Manually inspect all cross-split flagged pairs from original pixels and label ambiguous ones as such. A group with train+eval or train+val membership defeats independence at the field level even though filenames and image hashes differ. The release lacks verified well, plate, donor, acquisition-session and microscope-position metadata. Thus absence of a graph edge cannot prove independence. The existing eval has been seen and cannot be re-sealed; any group-aware metric here is exploratory. A new untouched acquisition cohort and paired published-model predictions remain required for a benchmark-win claim.
